ELECTRONICS · HANDS-ON BUILD

A start-and-stop timer game

A physical timing game built around a breadboard circuit and numeric display.

From a breadboard to a soldered board

I built a physical game where a player starts and stops a timer. The circuit uses pushbuttons for input and a three-digit, seven-segment display to show the time.

Building the electronics

The prototype photo shows the circuit developed on breadboards, with integrated circuits, resistors, LEDs, jumper wiring, and the numeric display. I worked through the wiring and tested the timing and display behavior as the circuit came together.

I then soldered all of the components onto the PCB. Moving from a breadboard to the soldered assembly meant making the connections permanent and checking the finished circuit carefully.

A complete physical game

The video shows the assembled game with its pushbuttons and display mounted in an enclosure. It was a satisfying example of taking electronics from a working circuit on the bench to something someone could pick up and play.
