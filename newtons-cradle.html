DOS PUEBLOS ENGINEERING ACADEMY · 2021–2022

Newton’s cradle

Redesigning the alignment and drive mechanisms of an interactive physics exhibit.

An interactive exhibit with a difficult linear stage

I worked with Kira and Trisha to improve a motorized Newton’s cradle at the Dos Pueblos Engineering Academy. Its motor-and-scoop assembly used lead screws to move horizontally and position the balls, but the stage had too much friction, the motion was slow, and parts of the structure flexed.

My main responsibility was the lead nut and its attachment and enclosure plates. The rotating lead screw passes through the nut; the nut is attached to the moving assembly so that screw rotation produces horizontal travel. Friction in that arrangement was preventing the scoop from moving smoothly.

Redesigning the nut and plate

I redesigned the lead nut with a triangular profile to provide more clearance, then modeled an attachment plate and an enclosure plate to fit and retain it. The hole pattern needed several revisions because the fasteners were too close to the plate edges.

Changing from #4 to #6 screws made the clearance problem more difficult. I extended the plate to create more space around the holes while preserving the existing interfaces. I also revised the opening in the half motor block so that it could accommodate the nut and be manufactured with an available drill size.

Machining and assembly problems

I machined the lead nut, worked on the plates, drilled an 11/16-inch hole in an aluminum half motor block, and faced and turned a steel lead screw to the required length and diameter. The long screw was difficult to support without wobble, so keeping it stable during machining was a practical challenge.

Updating the SolidWorks assembly brought its own problems: replacing the old plate created mating errors, and duplicated files in the design vault broke assembly references. I worked through those issues and rebuilt the assembly around the revised parts.

Disassembly was also more difficult than expected. Some screws were worn or secured in place, and bearings came out of the rails and had to be replaced carefully. These details mattered just as much as the CAD changes when it came time to rebuild the stage.

Alignment and exhibit testing

As a team, we replaced the lead screws to improve motion, added structural support, and made a jig that fit the exhibit slot to align the balls repeatably. I also machined and threaded spacers and modified clearance holes during final assembly.

We tested the mechanical improvements at the May 2022 engineering showcase. The control code still needed work, but the exhibit gave us a chance to see the changes operating as a complete system.
